Cooking temperature drives flavour loss

Hard candy is cooked to 150 degrees C and much of the volatile fraction boils off. Flavours for this application are formulated with higher-boiling components and are dosed after cooking, at the lowest temperature the process allows.

Interaction with acid and colour

Fruit flavours in candy are judged together with the acid system. A flavour balanced for 0.3 percent citric acid tastes flat at 0.8 percent, so the acid level is part of the flavour brief.

Typical Applications

  • Hard candy and lollipops — Added post-cook at 0.1-0.3 percent.
  • Gummy and chew — 0.2-0.5 percent, with the acid system matched to the flavour.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should flavour be added?

After cooking, below 90 degrees C, to limit volatile loss.

Can flavours be sugar-free compatible?

Yes, and they usually need extra masking for the polyol or high-intensity sweetener note.

Do you supply sample quantities?

Yes, 100-500 g samples for trials, with a lead time of about a week.
