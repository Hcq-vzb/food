RBD removes the coconut note

Refining, bleaching and deodorising strip the characteristic coconut flavour, which is exactly what a confectionery or creamer application needs. Virgin coconut oil would carry that note into the finished product.

Solid at room temperature, melts at body heat

The 24-26 degrees C melting point gives the snap and mouthfeel that coatings need, and it is why coconut oil is used in compound chocolate and ice cream coatings.

Typical Applications

  • Compound coatings — 20-35 percent as the lauric fat base for the snap and melt.
  • Non-dairy creamers — 3-8 percent as the fat source after spray drying.

Frequently Asked Questions

RBD or virgin?

RBD where a neutral taste is required; virgin where the coconut note is wanted.

Is it stable for frying?

It has good oxidative stability but a low smoke point relative to other frying oils, so check the process temperature.

How should it be stored?

Solid below 24 degrees C; keep sealed to avoid moisture pickup.
