Caseinate is a protein emulsifier, not a hydrocolloid

Sodium caseinate adsorbs at the oil-water interface and forms a strong film, which is why it stabilises coffee creamers and emulsified meat products better than most small-molecule emulsifiers. It also binds water, giving yield and texture.

Why creamers depend on it

In non-dairy creamer, caseinate provides whitening, mouthfeel and emulsion stability through drying and reconstitution. Attempts to replace it with vegetable protein usually need additional emulsifier and still lose whiteness.

Typical Applications

  • Coffee creamers — 2-5 percent gives whitening and a stable emulsion after spray drying.
  • Processed meat — 1-3 percent improves water binding and slice cohesion.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it a dairy allergen?

Yes, it is a milk protein and must be declared as milk on the label in most markets.

Can vegans use it?

No. Plant-based alternatives need a different protein and emulsifier system.

What is the difference from calcium caseinate?

Sodium caseinate dissolves readily and is used for emulsification; calcium caseinate disperses rather than dissolves and suits meat binding.
