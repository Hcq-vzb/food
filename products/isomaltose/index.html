A non-reducing sugar for sensitive systems

Isomaltose resists browning and acid hydrolysis better than maltose, which makes it useful where a product must stay pale and stable through long shelf life.

Specialty and fermentation use

Because it is relatively scarce and costly, it is used in specialty confectionery and as a defined substrate in fermentation and research applications rather than as a mainstream sweetener.

Typical Applications

  • Specialty confectionery — Where colour stability through storage is critical.
  • Fermentation media — A defined carbohydrate source for controlled microbial work.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is it different from isomalt?

Isomaltose is a disaccharide of two glucose units; isomalt (E953) is a sugar alcohol blend used as a bulk sweetener.

Is it cost effective as a sweetener?

No. Its use is functional, not economical sweetness.

Is it non-reducing?

Yes, which is why it resists Maillard browning.
